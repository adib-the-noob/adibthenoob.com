Most product interfaces try too hard. They shout for attention with gradients, motion, and novelty for its own sake. The result is noise.

A calm interface does three things well:

1. **Names the work clearly.** The user should know what this surface is for before reading a paragraph.
2. **Keeps hierarchy honest.** One primary action. Secondary paths stay secondary.
3. **Respects attention.** Every animation, accent, and badge has to earn its place.

<Callout title="Rule of thumb">
  If removing an accent, animation, or badge does not hurt comprehension, remove it.
</Callout>

Restraint is not a lack of ambition. It is a design decision about what matters. When you cut the cleverness, the product gets faster to learn and harder to misunderstand.

I build software with that bar in mind — clear systems under calm surfaces.
